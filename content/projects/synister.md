[@eckstein_2024_neurotransmitter]

High-resolution electron microscopy of nervous systems has enabled the
reconstruction of synaptic connectomes. However, we do not know the synaptic
sign for each connection (i.e., whether a connection is excitatory or
inhibitory), which is implied by the released neurotransmitter.

We demonstrate that artificial neural networks can predict transmitter types
for presynapses from electron micrographs: a network trained to predict six
transmitters (ACh, Glut, GABA, Ser, Dop, Oct) achieves an accuracy of 87% for
individual synapses and 94% for entire neurons.

We visualize the ultrastructural features used for prediction, discovering
subtle but significant differences between neurotransmitter phenotypes.

We also analyze neurotransmitter distributions across the brain and find that
neurons that develop together largely express only one fast-acting
neurotransmitter (ACh, Glut, or GABA).

We hope that our publicly available predictions act as an accelerant for
neuroscientific hypothesis generation for the fly.
